\section{Naïve Power Law Fit}
We hypothesize a power law scaling relationship for Toppling Size $S$ and area $A$ of the form:
\begin{align*}
    p(S=s; \tau, s_{max}) &\propto s^{-\tau_s}
    p(A=a; \tau) &\propto a^{-\tau_a}
\end{align*}

In the above, $s_{max}$ and $a_{max}$ are known constants.

Let $\mathbf{s} = (s_1, \cdots, s_T)^T, \mathbf{a} = (a_1, \cdots, a_T)^T$ be vectors representing the simulation data for topple size and area respectively.

The likelihood function for topple size (and similarly area) is:

\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{s}; \tau_s, s_{max}) &= \prod_{i=1}^T \frac{s_i^{-\tau_s}}{C_{\tau_s, s_{max}}} \\
    \log \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{s}; \tau_s, s_{max}) &= -T \log C_{\tau_s, s_{max}} -\tau_s \sum_{i=1}^T \log{s_i} \\
    \intertext{Where $C_{\tau_s, s_{max}}$ is the normalising constant:}
    C_{\tau_s, s_{max}} &= \sum_{k=1}^{s_{max}} k^{-\tau_s}
\end{align*}

To estimate the parameters $\tau_s$ and $\tau_a$, we compute their Maximum Likelihood Estimates (MLEs).
These are the values $\hat{\tau_s}$ and $\hat{\tau_a}$ that satisfy
\[\hat{\tau_s} = \argmax_{\tau_s} \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{s}; \tau_s, s_{max}) = \argmin_{\tau_s} \left\{- \log \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{s}; \tau_s, s_{max}) \right\}\]
and similarly for $\hat{\tau_a}$.

Because the normalising constant $C_{\tau_s, s_{max}}$ depends on the parameter $\tau_s$, we cannot find an analytic solution and rely on numerical optimisation techniques.
The numerical optimisation technique used here is the L-BFGS-B algorithm introduced by Byrd et al. (1995) \cite{byrd1995limited} and implemented in the \texttt{scipy} python library.

Initially, we ignore the non-linear tails of the distribution and model the power law as though it is valid for all plausible values.
For area, there is a natural upper bound $a_{max} = L^2$. 
For topple size, which is in theory unbounded, we choose an arbitrary upper bound $s_{max} = 2 L^2$ which exceeded all observed topple sizes to compute the normalising constant.

The estimates obtained are presented in Figure \ref{fig:powerlaw_taus} and Table \ref{tab:powerlaw_taus}.

However, inspection of the fit against data in Figure \ref{fig:powerlaw_fitted_mle} indicates that the estimated slope has been overestimated due to the non-powerlaw behaviour of the system at the tails.
This effect appears to be most prominent in smaller lattice sizes, but still visibly impacts the fit for $L=100$.

To avoid manually setting an arbitrary maximum for each value of $L$, here we consider the rule-of-thumb approximation $s_{max}=a_{max}= 0.1 L^2$.
